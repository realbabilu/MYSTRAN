# DSG3 Triangular Shell Element: Three-Ordering Discrete Shear Gaps and Objective Consistent-Spin Drilling

**Ary | 25 October 2026 | Mathematical formulation and numerical assessment**

## Abstract

A six-degree-of-freedom three-node Reissner–Mindlin triangular shell formulation is presented. It combines a constant-strain triangular membrane, a linear compatible bending field, a three-ordering discrete shear gap (DSG) energy construction, and an objective drilling-rotation constraint coupling nodal drilling to the continuum membrane spin. The three-ordering shear construction removes the additional zero-energy plate mechanism seen in a single-reference-node DSG formulation in the examined element-rank test. The consistent-spin constraint replaces the formerly independent diagonal drilling penalty. Numerical comparisons demonstrate a substantial improvement on the hemispherical shell benchmark 2-007 (absolute error 78.043% to 1.047% at the specified refined mesh), while revealing nonuniform convergence on the twisted beam 2-004, particularly LC1 at n24 (4.304% to 10.161%). An energy decomposition, coefficient sensitivity study, element-frame invariance tests, and nodal-director objectivity study are used to assess the origin and implications of the trade-off. The adopted formulation retains the facet-based consistent-spin constraint with coefficient $\alpha_s=10^{-3}$; trial parameter changes and nodal-director substitutions are reported as sensitivity investigations, not as adopted modifications.

**Keywords:** discrete shear gap; triangular shell; Reissner–Mindlin; drilling rotation; consistent spin; rigid-body objectivity; twisted beam; hemispherical shell.

## 1. Introduction

The discrete shear gap approach of Bletzinger, Bischoff and Ramm [1] reconstructs transverse shear strain from line-integrated displacement–rotation kinematics. Its chief motivation is to alleviate transverse-shear locking in low-order Reissner–Mindlin elements. A triangular shell with three nodes must additionally control mechanisms generated by incomplete membrane, bending and transverse-shear approximations. Rotations about the element normal (*drilling rotations*) require special treatment because conventional constant-strain membrane and Mindlin plate operators do not provide physical drilling stiffness.

The formulation examined here is characterized by **two independent design choices**: (i) the DSG shear stiffness is formed from the mean of the **energies** of three cyclic reference-node operators, and (ii) drilling stiffness penalizes the difference between nodal normal rotations and in-plane continuum spin. The second choice is physically more consistent than a diagonal penalty on absolute nodal drilling rotations. The resulting shell is a flat-facet model expressed in a right-handed local frame.

The numerical comparisons are reported as **element-formulation results**. A distinction is maintained between the selected formulation, the historical comparison formulation, and experimental alternatives that were evaluated but not adopted.

## 2. Geometry and kinematics

Consider a nondegenerate three-node triangular facet with global coordinates $\mathbf{x}_1$, $\mathbf{x}_2$, and $\mathbf{x}_3$. Define a right-handed orthonormal local basis by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf e_1 &= \frac{\mathbf x_2-\mathbf x_1}{\|\mathbf x_2-\mathbf x_1\|},\\
\mathbf e_3 &= \frac{(\mathbf x_2-\mathbf x_1)\times(\mathbf x_3-\mathbf x_1)}{\|(\mathbf x_2-\mathbf x_1)\times(\mathbf x_3-\mathbf x_1)\|},\\
\mathbf e_2 &= \mathbf e_3\times\mathbf e_1.
\end{aligned}
$$

The triangular area is $A>0$, and its local shape functions are

$$
N_1=1-r-s,\qquad N_2=r,\qquad N_3=s.
$$

Each node has local generalized displacement vector

$$
\mathbf d_i=\begin{bmatrix}u_i & v_i & w_i & \theta_{xi} & \theta_{yi} & \theta_{zi}\end{bmatrix}^{T},
\qquad \mathbf d\in\mathbb R^{18}.
$$

The local-to-global orthogonal transformation acts on **both** translations and rotations. The positive drilling rotation is the component along $\mathbf e_3$. Consistent right-hand-rule signs are used in membrane spin and nodal rotations.

## 3. Constitutive matrices

For homogeneous isotropic plane stress, define

$$
\mathbf C_0=\frac{E}{1-\nu^2}
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & \nu & 0 \\
\nu & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \dfrac{1-\nu}{2}
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The membrane, bending and transverse-shear constitutive matrices are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf D_m &= h\mathbf C_0,\\
\mathbf D_b &= \frac{h^3}{12}\mathbf C_0,\\
\mathbf D_s &= \kappa_s Gh\mathbf I_2,\\
G &= \frac{E}{2(1+\nu)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $h$ is thickness and $\kappa_s$ is the transverse-shear correction factor. The constitutive factors already include the appropriate thickness powers; they must **not** be multiplied by thickness a second time when forming the element energies.

## 4. Compatible membrane and bending operators

With $N_{i,x}$ and $N_{i,y}$ denoting derivatives in the local facet plane, the membrane strains are

$$
\boldsymbol\varepsilon_m=\mathbf B_m\mathbf d
=\begin{bmatrix}u_{,x} & v_{,y} & u_{,y}+v_{,x}\end{bmatrix}^{T}.
$$

For each node, its membrane contribution in the standard six-DOF ordering is

$$
\mathbf B_{m,i}=
\begin{bmatrix}
N_{i,x}&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&N_{i,y}&0&0&0&0\\
N_{i,y}&N_{i,x}&0&0&0&0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The linear Mindlin bending operator corresponding to a right-handed rotation convention can be expressed as

$$
\mathbf B_{b,i}=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&0&0&0&N_{i,x}&0\\
0&0&0&-N_{i,y}&0&0\\
0&0&0&-N_{i,x}&N_{i,y}&0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Thus

$$
\mathbf K_m=A\mathbf B_m^T\mathbf D_m\mathbf B_m,\qquad
\mathbf K_b=A\mathbf B_b^T\mathbf D_b\mathbf B_b.
$$

The drilling degrees of freedom do not enter these two compatible operators directly. Their energetic contribution is introduced separately in Section 6.

## 5. Three-ordering discrete shear gap formulation

Let $\sigma$ denote one of the three cyclic node orderings $(1,2,3)$, $(2,3,1)$ and $(3,1,2)$. For a chosen starting node, the standard discrete shear gap construction integrates the Reissner–Mindlin transverse-shear kinematics along element edges, producing a constant assumed-shear operator $\mathbf B_s^{(\sigma)}$. Denoting $\mathbf d$ as the element displacement vector,

$$
\widetilde{\boldsymbol\gamma}^{(\sigma)}
=\mathbf B_s^{(\sigma)}\mathbf d.
$$

The proposed shear energy is the arithmetic mean of the three positive-semidefinite quadratic energies:

$$
\boxed{
\Pi_s(\mathbf d)=\frac{A}{6}
\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3}
\mathbf d^T\mathbf B_s^{(\sigma)T}\mathbf D_s\mathbf B_s^{(\sigma)}\mathbf d.
}
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf K_s=\frac{A}{3}\sum_{\sigma=1}^{3}
\mathbf B_s^{(\sigma)T}\mathbf D_s\mathbf B_s^{(\sigma)}.
}
$$

**Energy averaging is not strain-operator averaging:** in general,

$$
\frac{1}{3}\sum_{\sigma}\mathbf B_s^{(\sigma)T}\mathbf D_s\mathbf B_s^{(\sigma)}
\ne
\overline{\mathbf B}_s^T\mathbf D_s\overline{\mathbf B}_s,
\qquad
\overline{\mathbf B}_s=\frac13\sum_{\sigma}\mathbf B_s^{(\sigma)}.
$$

The three-ordering construction has a well-defined **quadratic discrete energy functional** and therefore produces a symmetric stiffness matrix. It is not necessary to claim that this construction is nonvariational. A stricter question—whether it is the exact discretization of a particular single-field continuous Reissner–Mindlin functional—remains separate.

### 5.1 Element rank and reference-node invariance

In the audited isolated-element test, a single-ordering DSG shear construction retains an additional zero-energy plate mechanism. Averaging the three shear energies removes this mechanism. The plate-related nine generalized DOFs ($w,\theta_x,\theta_y$ at three nodes) have three rigid modes and hence six expected deformational modes. The examined results were:

| Isolated plate block | Stiffness rank | Nullity |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bending + single-reference DSG shear | 5 / 9 | 4 (three rigid + one spurious) |
| Bending + three-ordering DSG shear | **6 / 9** | **3 (rigid only)** |

With compatible membrane, shear, bending and objective drilling assembled,  18-DOF shell exhibits six rigid-body modes in the numerical rank check. The number of vanishing eigenvalues depends on the stiffness blocks present and must not be conflated with the rank of the isolated plate subblock.

## 6. Objective consistent-spin drilling stabilization

### 6.1 Definition

The historical diagonal drilling form penalized nodal $\theta_z$ independently.  formulation instead compares drilling rotation with the local continuum spin of the membrane displacement field. Define

$$
\omega_{xy}=\frac12(v_{,x}-u_{,y}),
\qquad
\theta_z(r,s)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}N_i(r,s)\theta_{zi}.
$$

The scalar consistent-spin constraint is

$$
\boxed{
\gamma_d(r,s)=\omega_{xy}-\theta_z(r,s).
}
$$

Its discrete operator $\mathbf b_d(r,s)$, in the six-DOF nodal ordering, has per-node entries

$$
\mathbf b_{d,i}(r,s)=
\begin{bmatrix}
-\dfrac12N_{i,y} & \dfrac12N_{i,x} & 0 & 0 & 0 & -N_i(r,s)
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

For symmetric triangular integration points $(r_g,s_g)$ and normalized weights $w_g=1/3$,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf K_d=\alpha_s GhA
\sum_{g=1}^{3}w_g\,
\mathbf b_d(r_g,s_g)^T\mathbf b_d(r_g,s_g),
\qquad \alpha_s=10^{-3}.
}
$$

The adopted integration points are

$$
\mathbf{C}_0 =
\frac{E}{1-\nu^2}
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & \nu & 0 \\
\nu & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \dfrac{1-\nu}{2}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{D}_m &= h\mathbf{C}_0, \\
\mathbf{D}_b &= \frac{h^3}{12}\mathbf{C}_0, \\
\mathbf{D}_s &= \kappa_s Gh\mathbf{I}_2
\end{aligned}
$$

The selected stabilized local stiffness is

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf K_e=\mathbf K_m+\mathbf K_b+\mathbf K_s+\mathbf K_d.
}
$$

The coefficient $\alpha_s$ scales stabilization energy and is not an elastic constitutive constant; Section 9 reports its sensitivity.

### 6.2 Rigid-body objectivity

For an infinitesimal rigid rotation $\boldsymbol\Omega$, the nodal translations satisfy $\mathbf u_i=\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf x_i$ and nodal rotations satisfy $\boldsymbol\theta_i=\boldsymbol\Omega$. On a planar triangular facet,

$$
\omega_{xy}=\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\mathbf e_3,
\qquad
\sum_iN_i\theta_{zi}=\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\mathbf e_3.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{\gamma_d^{\rm RBM}=0,\qquad \Pi_d^{\rm RBM}=0.}
$$

A penalty on the **absolute** nodal drilling rotation does not satisfy this property in general. Consistent-spin stabilization removes that artificial rigid-motion coupling while retaining a positive-semidefinite drilling contribution.

## 8. Operator/frame consistency checks

The element-level operator was evaluated after cyclic and reversed node permutations, followed by reordering to the common global nodal sequence. Normalized stiffness discrepancies and rigid-motion residuals were:

| Geometric sample | Maximum cyclic error | Maximum reversed error | Maximum rigid-mode residual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2-004 n12, 32 facets | $1.15\times10^{-15}$ | $1.15\times10^{-15}$ | $1.25\times10^{-16}$ |
| 2-004 n24, 32 facets | $7.40\times10^{-16}$ | $7.98\times10^{-16}$ | $1.01\times10^{-16}$ |
| 2-007 n8, 32 facets | $6.93\times10^{-16}$ | $1.03\times10^{-15}$ | $1.16\times10^{-16}$ |

The observed near-machine-precision errors support correct element-level basis transformations and orientation handling for these samples. They do **not** establish a full curved-shell convergence theorem or every possible distorted-element patch condition.

Adjacent facet-normal jumps in the twisted beam decrease with refinement:

| Twisted-beam mesh | Median adjacent-normal angle |
|---|---:|
| n8 | 5.983° |
| n12 | 4.273° |
| n16 | 3.482° |
| n24 | 2.781° |

Accordingly, the n24 overshoot cannot be attributed simply to a larger discontinuity in facet normals on finer meshes.

The lowest maximum error among the **eight sampled 2-004 observables** occurs at $\alpha_s=0.003$, whereas $\alpha_s=0.010$ yields the lowest unweighted average error in that restricted sweep. Neither criterion establishes an optimal coefficient across the complete shell benchmark family. The adopted $10^{-3}$ baseline is retained instead of tuning a parameter against a single benchmark.

### 9.2 Drilling strain energy

The drilling energy is evaluated at the equilibrated displacement field $\mathbf d(\alpha_s)$:

$$
\eta_d(\alpha_s)=
\frac{\frac12\mathbf d^T\mathbf K_d(\alpha_s)\mathbf d}
{\frac12\mathbf d^T\mathbf K_e(\alpha_s)\mathbf d}
\times100\%.
$$

| Case | $\eta_d$ at $\alpha_s=0.001$ | $\eta_d$ at $\alpha_s=0.010$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| LC1 n12 | 2.176% | 2.055% |
| LC1 n24 | 5.605% | 2.580% |
| LC2 n12 | 2.351% | 2.314% |
| LC2 n24 | 6.440% | 3.108% |

Increasing the coefficient tenfold can **decrease** the fraction of equilibrated strain energy stored in the drilling constraint because the displacement field itself changes. At n24, the decreasing drilling fraction is consistent with a reduced spin mismatch, but it is not evidence of a universal improvement in shell accuracy.

## 10. Nodal-director investigation and objectivity

An averaged nodal director $\mathbf n_i$ can be constructed from surrounding facet normals, but substituting it directly for the facet normal in the drilling constraint is not automatically objective. The facet-based constraint is

$$
\gamma_d^{f}=\omega_{xy}-\sum_i N_i\bigl(\boldsymbol\theta_i\cdot\mathbf n_f\bigr).
$$

A naive nodal-director alternative would be

$$
\gamma_d^{n}=\omega_{xy}-\sum_i N_i\bigl(\boldsymbol\theta_i\cdot\mathbf n_i\bigr).
$$

Under a rigid rotation $\boldsymbol\Omega$, it gives

$$
\boxed{
\gamma_d^{n,\rm RBM}=
\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\left(\mathbf n_f-\sum_iN_i\mathbf n_i\right),
}
$$

which generally does not vanish on a faceted curved surface. Sampled maximum unit-rotation mismatch amplitudes were 0.04356 (twisted n12), 0.02387 (twisted n24), and 0.06212 (hemisphere n8). These numbers are **spin-constraint mismatches**, not equilibrium residuals. The naive substitution is therefore rejected independently of its benchmark displacement results.

A rigid-motion-exact relative-rotation director correction was also evaluated:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{\boldsymbol\theta} &= \sum_iN_i\boldsymbol\theta_i,\\
\gamma_d^{\rm trial} &= \gamma_d^{f}+
\sum_iN_i(\boldsymbol\theta_i-\overline{\boldsymbol\theta})
\cdot(\mathbf n_f-\mathbf n_i).
\end{aligned}
$$

The additional term is zero for rigid rotations. Nevertheless, its static benchmark changes were negligible or unfavorable:

| Benchmark | Selected facet spin error | Objective director trial error |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2-004 LC1 n12 | 1.052% | **0.990%** |
| **2-004 LC1 n24** | **10.161%** | 10.192% |
| 2-004 LC2 n24 | **16.389%** | 16.426% |
| 2-007 hemisphere n8 | **4.188%** | 4.219% |
| 2-007 hemisphere n32 | 1.0468% | **1.0465%** |
| 2-006 Scordelis n16 | 2.0432% | **2.0402%** |

The objective trial did not resolve the twisted-beam n24 overshoot. It is not part of the selected formulation. A truly covariant curved-shell treatment would also need compatible membrane/bending/shear director gradients, beyond a drilling-only projection correction.

## 11. Discussion and limitations

The principal result is that the **objective facet-based consistent-spin** contribution corrects an extreme hemispherical-shell under-response while maintaining near-identical results for standard plate load cases. Its residual twisted-beam response is not uniformly superior to the historical drilling treatment. On the fine twisted LC1 mesh, the coefficient sweep confirms sensitivity to the drilling strength, but a coefficient chosen for one mesh can transfer error to coarser meshes.

The rank and permutation tests support the consistency of the isolated element but do not prove all patch-test requirements or a curved-surface asymptotic consistency statement. A remaining hypothesis is that the facet-shell membrane/bending kinematics and their interaction with drilling under twist may control the observed convergence pattern. Current evidence does not uniquely isolate these mechanisms. It would be incorrect to declare a new covariant shell formulation on the basis of the rejected director-only experiments.

## 12. Conclusions

1. Three-ordering **shear-energy averaging** creates a symmetric, positive-semidefinite shear contribution and removes the tested isolated plate spurious mode; it should not be confused with averaging the strain operators.
2. Consistent-spin drilling constrains nodal rotations against the in-plane continuum spin, preserving the six infinitesimal rigid-body modes in the examined element tests.
3. The selected $\alpha_s=10^{-3}$ formulation substantially improves hemisphere 2-007 n32 but exhibits known regressions on twisted-beam LC1 n24 and very coarse Scordelis–Lo geometry.
4. Scalar drilling retuning can substantially improve a chosen mesh, yet does not give uniform improvement over the tested refinement levels.
5. Naive nodal-normal replacement violates rigid-body objectivity. An objective relative-rotation correction restores rigid-motion consistency but brings no material improvement on the critical twisted-beam case.
6. The appropriate scientific interpretation is a demonstrably improved but **not universally superior** flat-facet DSG3 shell formulation; unresolved twisted-geometry convergence is retained as an explicit limitation.
